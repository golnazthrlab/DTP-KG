Comparison of shared protein neighborhoods for a high-scoring positive and a low-scoring reliable-negative drug pair. Left: trabectedin (DB05109, seen)–teriflunomide (DB08880, held out), a reference-positive pair with topology-only prediction score 0.944; their 46- and 38-protein neighborhoods share six proteins. Right: carmustine (DB00262, seen)–brinzolamide (DB01194, held out), a reliable-negative pair with score 0.080; their 34- and 49-protein neighborhoods share one protein. Neighborhoods include recorded direct targets and their immediate protein–protein interaction (PPI) neighbors, at most two edges from each drug. Neither pair shares direct targets: every red protein is reached indirectly from both drugs through a drug–target–protein route. Blue and orange identify the seen and held-out drug neighborhoods; red identifies shared proteins. Squares represent drugs, diamonds their direct targets, and circles other proteins. Drug names/IDs, shared proteins and direct targets on the saved connecting routes are labelled; short grey leaders connect labels to their nodes. Darker node colours and coloured drug–target edges distinguish direct connections from the paler surrounding proteins. All 78 and 82 distinct proteins are displayed, respectively, using identical marker sizes and common axis limits. Shared nodes are aligned centrally for readability; positions are schematic. Edges show drug–target connections and PPI context within either neighborhood. Both pairs come from the same DDI-free seen–unseen evaluation fit (split 00, seed 101). They were selected post hoc for contrasting overlap at high/low scores, with the negative chosen for similar endpoint neighborhood sizes. These are descriptive illustrations of multihop protein context, not verified pathway membership or independent evidence for a general overlap–score relationship.
